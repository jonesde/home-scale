## 1. Core Methodology

Quantum Systems Analysis (QSA) models quantum entities as finite state machines (or deterministic, rule-bound systems) whose complete internal configuration and constraints, once known, yield full predictability of behavior.

Apparent randomness or non-determinism is treated as the result of incomplete knowledge of those internal rules, node states, couplings, and environmental constraints.

Macroscopic “laws,” constants, and stable effects are treated as the averaged, synchronized output of large numbers of these constrained microscopic state machines.

The method proceeds by reverse-engineering the effective machine specifications from experimental boundaries: what the system can and cannot do, and the precise thresholds at which macroscopic effects appear or collapse.

No language of intentional design, agency, purpose, or external programmer is used or required.

---

## 2. Foundational Principles

### 2.1 Constraint Reasoning
Deduce the internal rules and resource limits of the state machines by analyzing:
- what the system cannot do, and
- the exact threshold (spatial, energetic, thermal, temporal, or mechanical) at which the macroscopic effect fails.

### 2.2 Emergent Architecture
Treat macroscopic laws and constants not as bedrock primitives, but as the stable, averaged output generated by large numbers of synchronized microscopic routines operating under shared constraints.

### 2.3 Performance Metrics
Quantify the operational limits of the quantum media using established experimental boundaries. Primary metrics include:
- minimum hardware scale / node count / coherence volume (“buffer size”),
- clock rates and response latencies,
- energy barriers or thresholds that protect a state,
- error / failure envelopes (thermal, mechanical, electromagnetic, etc.).

---

## 3. Purpose of the Effect Library

The library is the primary instrument for testing the QSA approach on its own terms:
- **Utility** — Does the mapping produce clear, usable performance specifications?
- **Internal consistency** — Do the extracted constraints, scales, and failure modes form a coherent picture under the finite-state-machine + full-predictability-once-understood assumption?

Each entry is a self-contained artifact. No entry asserts claims beyond the data and the mapping. Inconsistencies, gaps, or failures of the mapping are recorded explicitly.

---

## 4. Library Entry Template

Every entry in the library shall follow this fixed structure.

### 4.1 Effect Name
Concise, conventional name of the phenomenon.

### 4.2 Macroscopic Observation
What is directly measured or experienced at the macroscopic scale. Keep this purely observational.

### 4.3 Established Quantitative Boundaries
Key numerical thresholds, scaling relations, and experimental limits.
Include a confidence rating for the data themselves (High / Medium / Low) based on reproducibility and precision of the measurements.

### 4.4 Standard Mathematical Description
The principal equations, parameters, or formal relations used in the literature.
These are recorded strictly as data that describe observed behavior; they are not treated as foundational axioms.

### 4.5 QSA Mapping
- **Minimum hardware scale / node count / coherence volume** required for the macroscopic effect to appear and remain stable.
- **Clock rate / latency** of the relevant microscopic response (derived from energy scales, plasma frequencies, precession rates, etc.).
- **Energy barriers or thresholds** that protect the operating state.
- **Dominant failure modes** and the precise constraints whose violation collapses the macroscopic effect.
- **Emergent constant or stable output** produced by the synchronized machines.

### 4.6 Internal Consistency Notes
Does the mapping close cleanly under the working assumptions?
Record any gaps, tensions, missing constraints, or places where the finite-state-machine picture does not yet account for the data.

### 4.7 Overall Confidence in the QSA Mapping
High / Medium / Low, with a one-sentence justification focused on how well the extracted specifications fit the observed boundaries and failure modes.

---

## 5. Working Rules for Library Construction

1. Every quantitative claim must be traceable to experimental boundaries or well-established measured parameters.
2. Language remains limited to constraints, resources, synchronization, failure thresholds, and emergent output.
3. Interpretive overlays that are not required by the data are omitted.
4. When the mapping is incomplete, the incompleteness is stated explicitly rather than filled by conjecture.
5. Entries may be revised as better data or tighter constraint analysis becomes available.
